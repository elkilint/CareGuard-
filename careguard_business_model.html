CareGuard · Business model & pricing
B2C · Mid-range · Latin America, US & Canada
Business model
Hardware + recurring subscription — the "razor and blade" model
CareGuard earns revenue on two layers: a one-time hardware sale (the sensor kit) that gets the family committed, and a monthly or annual subscription that generates predictable recurring revenue. The hardware is priced at or near cost to lower the adoption barrier. The subscription is where the business lives. This mirrors what ADT, Ring, and Apple One do — the device is the hook, the service is the engine.

Geographic pricing strategy
🇺🇸

Full USD pricing. Primary growth market. Latino diaspora families caring for parents abroad are a high-fit, high-willingness-to-pay segment.
🇨🇦

CAD pricing at parity with USD tiers. Strong immigrant population with family in LATAM. Similar purchasing power — full pricing applies.
🌎
Latin America
Localized pricing at 50–60% of USD rates. Colombia, Mexico, Chile as launch countries. Local payment methods: PSE, OXXO, Mercado Pago.
Competitive moat
🧠
Personalized AI that gets smarter over time
The longer a family uses CareGuard, the more accurate the AI becomes. A competitor can copy the hardware — they can't copy 18 months of behavioral data on your grandfather.
👨‍👩‍👧
Multi-member family network effect
Every family member added is a new distribution node. Families refer CareGuard to siblings and friends in the same situation — CAC drops as the network grows.
🌎
Cross-border family positioning
No competitor is built for the Latino diaspora dynamic — a child in the US caring for a parent in Colombia. This binational emotional context is a defensible niche that global players won't prioritize.
🔒
Privacy-first as a trust asset
No cameras, no voice recording. In a market where surveillance anxiety is real, CareGuard's architecture is a marketing differentiator — not just an ethical choice.
